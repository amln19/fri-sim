04 / METHOD, LIMITATIONS & TRANSFER

Inspect the experiment.

Explicit assumptions, reproducible records, and a defined interface for the physical-car extension.

Information boundary

Normal planning receives only delivered timestamped messages and its own issued commands. The renderer and evaluator have ground truth. A shared, optional local-range emergency controller uses clearance and closing motion within 1.2 m; its interventions are logged for every policy.

Human scored trials show received traffic state. Observer practice shows ground truth. The tall column is a presentation occluder, not a modeled sensor available to the automated coordinator.

Shared model

SI units at full vehicle scale. Vehicles are 4.4 × 1.85 m, circle radius 13 m, acceleration 2.4 m/s², braking 4.5 m/s², nominal cruise 9 m/s. Physics runs at 60 Hz; V2V at 10 Hz; coordination at 5 Hz. All four fixed routes conservatively reserve the same circular conflict region, including a half-body allowance at entry and exit.

Candidate scheduling

Enumerate 24 orders and greedily select each vehicle’s earliest feasible release on a common 0.5-second grid over 45 seconds. Minimize the predicted sum of approach waits across these candidates. Tie-break by vehicle ID. Preserve committed releases; hold when no supported schedule exists. This is not a globally optimal continuous-time solver.

Prediction & calibration

The route-relative predictor projects the last reported position using reported speed and message age, then applies the nominal controller to estimate entry and exit. It does not receive unreported motion disturbances. The joint score is max(|entry − predicted entry|, |exit − predicted exit|), in seconds.

Split-conformal rank: ceil((n + 1)(1 − α)). Unsupported ranks yield an infinite margin. Age bins are fixed at 0–200, 200–500, and 500–1200 ms; each needs at least 30 calibration encounters. Sparse adjacent bins are merged before testing, with the merged membership retained in the audit. Unsupported ages withhold new releases.

Calibration probes span held and moving pre-entry snapshots under recorded, fixed future release commands. They are independent encounters, not consecutive frames. Closed-loop evaluation changes the state distribution; its empirical coverage must be checked rather than assumed.

Fixed-margin acceptance

Search q ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4} s. Select the smallest tested margin with zero observed development collisions, near-conflict rate ≤ 10%, and full completion. A failure to qualify remains visible; finite development results do not establish zero risk.

Metrics & analysis

Approach wait starts at the fixed waiting-zone boundary, s = 0, and ends at first conflict entry, including slow travel. Initial offsets are already inside this zone at t = 0. Unentered vehicles have censored observed waits at termination. Report total and maximum individual wait.

Collision means overlapping oriented footprints. Near-conflict means positive body clearance below the configured threshold, and may occur in an encounter that also later collides. Minimum clearance is sampled at 60 Hz. Completion requires all vehicles to reach their route ends.

Event rates include all encounters and Wilson 95% intervals. Paired waiting differences resample complete seed pairs. Failed runs are retained; common completion counts accompany waiting comparisons. Conditions and thresholds are frozen in each exported audit.

Exploration settings

Loss/jitter apply to live exploration only. Changes to dynamics, emergency behavior, alpha, or near-conflict threshold invalidate existing calibration for new runs.

1/10-scale hardware interface

The browser exposes window.RoundaboutLab: the deterministic engine, current message/command envelopes, and configuration export. Normal coordination has a received-state boundary; physics and rendering are separate.

State message
{ vehicle, sequence, generated,
  receipt, s, v, route }

Release command
{ t, vehicle, type: "release",
  packet_sequence, packet_generated,
  age, interval: { entry, exit, q } }

This is a software interface, not a connection to physical cars. Hardware needs an adapter, a clock-synchronization design, localization and route mapping, validated scale/dynamics, and separate calibration. Simulator results do not establish hardware performance.

Storage & reproducibility

Data stays in this browser. Download pilot CSVs, comparison results, and full JSON audits. Original pilot storage is read only and has its own export. Recordings are memory-only until downloaded. No participant or experiment data is sent to a server.
